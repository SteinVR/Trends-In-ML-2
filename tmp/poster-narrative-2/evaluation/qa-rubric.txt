POSTER COMPARISON RUBRIC

Hard gates
- Exactly one DIN A1 landscape page (841 x 594 mm; aspect ratio 1.4158).
- Visible sections: Hypothesis, Methodology, Results.
- Paragraph, chart-label, and reference text is at least 24 pt.
- No clipping, unintended overlap, broken glyphs, or unresolved placeholders.
- No invented author/student ID or unsupported scientific claim.
- 0.847 is labelled as benchmark grounding on 100 answers.
- 0.892/89.2% is labelled as macro page F-beta on 95 annotated questions.
- Multiplicative score is exactly 0.956 x 0.847 x 1.000 x 0.938 = 0.759.
- Attribution effect: +10.2 pp precision, -0.33 pages/question, 95/95 hit coverage.
- Boundary effect: single-page +4.0 pp, multi-page -2.6 pp, single-document +3.2 pp, multi-document -1.4 pp.
- Mean TTFT is 6.39 s and is not described as efficient without a baseline.
- Editable/native source, final PDF, high-resolution PNG, A4-scale preview, and QA notes exist.

Visual score (0-5 each)
1. Thirty-second comprehension: research question, total score/bottleneck, attribution gain, and multi-page regression are all findable.
2. Reading order: title -> hypothesis -> pipeline -> evidence -> decision works without oral explanation.
3. Evidence hierarchy: result numbers dominate prose; comparisons and denominators are unambiguous.
4. Method clarity: page provenance visibly persists through the pipeline, without implying component ablation.
5. Typography: readable at full A1 and useful at A4 preview; no paragraph is solved by shrinking type.
6. Composition: balanced density, disciplined alignment, and no dashboard-card monotony.
7. Chart craft: equation strip, attribution comparison, and zero-centred boundary bars encode the right relationships.
8. Scientific honesty: partial support, uncertainty, limitations, and distributed-evidence regression remain prominent.
9. Production quality: clean PDF, crisp SVG/native vectors, sufficient raster resolution, reliable reproduction.
10. Distinctiveness: a memorable visual thesis that remains academic and avoids legal-tech cliches.

Selection rule
- Any hard-gate failure disqualifies a candidate until corrected.
- Among passing candidates, prefer the highest visual score; if scores are within two points, prefer the more editable and reproducible source.
