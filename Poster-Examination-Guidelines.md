# Research Poster

## Summary

The poster must be based on empirical work. The following guidelines define all requirements. Read them carefully.

## Poster

The poster is the core submission. It must present empirical work in the field of NLP, structured into the following three mandatory sections.

### Hypothesis

State your research questions clearly. Situate your work within the broader research context, explaining what prior work exists and where your contribution fits.

### Methodology

Describe and justify the methods you employed, including the datasets, models, and algorithms selected for your task. If your research involves technical implementations, you must provide a link to an external repository (e.g. GitLab or GitHub) that allows us to reproduce your experiments.

### Results

Present your results using tabular and/or graphical representations. Discuss your findings with respect to your original hypothesis, including any limitations or unexpected outcomes.

### Technical requirements

In addition, your poster must meet the following technical requirements:

- **Format:** DIN A1 (594 × 841 mm), portrait or landscape orientation.
- **Text:** Readable in terms of color (sufficient contrast to background objects) and large enough for a poster; paragraphs should be at least 24 pt.
- **Figures:** Readable in terms of resolution; pixel images should have at least 150 PPI.

> ⚠️ While the scientific content is our main grading criterion, we will also assess design and layout aspects, including readability, spacing, and visual concept. The design should strengthen your argument and content rather than serve as meaningless decoration.

## Appendix

The appendix is a separate, mandatory PDF document accompanying your poster. It must include the following two components.

### References

Provide a complete list of all sources consulted. References must primarily be academic papers. A collection consisting solely of website links does not qualify as a valid reference list.

Non-academic sources are valid references in specific cases, including but not limited to:

- dataset documentation or data papers;
- language model technical reports or model cards;
- news articles relevant to your topic; and
- official software or tool documentation.
